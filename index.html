Years in Mumbai
Heritage

Rooted in Faith, Built for Service

The Methodist mission in Mumbai traces its roots to 1856, when William Taylor arrived in India. By 1864, the India Mission Conference was organized. The 1870 revival under Taylor established a foundation that would shape decades of growth.

Today, the Mumbai Regional Conference encompasses 34 congregations across South Bombay, North Mumbai, Pune, Thane, Navi Mumbai, Nagpur, and Nanded — continuing a legacy of worship, education, and compassionate service that began over 170 years ago.

1856

Arrival of William Taylor

Methodist revivalist William Taylor arrives in Bombay, beginning a movement that would shape the Christian community across Western India.

1864

India Mission Conference

The India Mission Conference is formally organized, establishing structured Methodist governance across the subcontinent.

1870

Taylor's Revival Movement

William Taylor returns to lead a powerful revival across India, strengthening Methodist congregations and drawing hundreds to faith.

1889

Bowen Memorial Built

The Bowen Memorial Methodist Church rises on Lytton Road, Colaba — a landmark of Gothic Revival architecture and enduring worship in South Bombay.

Explore Bowen Memorial →
1893

Bombay Annual Conference

The Bombay Annual Conference is established, marking formal Methodist organization in the Mumbai region with circuits organized by language.

1930

Chitambar — First Indian Bishop

Though Ramakrishna Gopal Chitambar served from 1914, his formal recognition as Bishop of the South India area (1930) marks a milestone — the first Indian to lead the Methodist Church in India.

1981

Autonomy — Methodist Church in India

On 7 January 1981 in Madras, the Methodist Church in India becomes a fully autonomous church — no longer a mission but a self-governing body of 2,460 congregations and 648,000 members.

2026

38th Session — MRC

The 38th session of the Mumbai Regional Conference convenes, continuing the mission of worship, education, and compassionate service that began 170 years ago.

Episcopal Leadership

Guided by Faith

Current Bishop

Bishop Dr. Newton M. Parmar

Bishop Dr. Newton M. Parmar serves as the current Bishop of the Mumbai Regional Conference of the Methodist Church in India. Under his leadership, the MRC continues to serve 34 congregations with approximately 30,000 members across South Bombay, North Mumbai, Pune, Thane, Navi Mumbai, Nagpur, and Nanded.

The episcopal succession includes some of the most influential leaders in Indian Methodism — from Chitambar, the first Indian bishop, to the 2026 session, a continuous line of spiritual leadership.
