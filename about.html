The Beginning

Methodism Comes to India

The story of the Methodist Church in India begins in 1856, when American Methodist Episcopal missionary William Butler arrived in India, beginning his work in the Bareilly/Lucknow region of North India.

The Indian uprising of 1857 interrupted the work, but the mission resumed. By 1864, the work had grown substantially enough to form the India Mission Conference.

However, the real transformation came in 1870, when evangelist William Taylor was invited to India for revival meetings. Taylor's ministry produced Methodist congregations in Bombay, Poona, Calcutta, and other cities — transforming Methodism from an Upper India mission into a national movement.

Our Ancestry

The Bombay-Bengal Mission

In 1873, the churches established through William Taylor's revival work were organized into the Bombay-Bengal Mission. This is the most important date for MRC history — it marks the formal beginning of the institutional chain that leads directly to today's Mumbai Regional Conference.

Bombay was not merely another regional outpost. It was one of the major urban centers through which Methodism expanded. The work in Bombay and Poona was central to Taylor's expansion, and the Bombay Conference subsequently became the ancestor of today's MRC.

What makes MRC particularly interesting is its multilingual character. The conference encompasses congregations in Tamil, English, Marathi, Kannada, Gujarati, and Hindustani — making it a truly multilingual metropolitan Methodist ecosystem.

Episcopal Seat

Taylor Memorial Methodist Church

Located on Clare Road, Byculla, Taylor Memorial Methodist Church is the episcopal seat of the Resident Bishop of the Mumbai Regional Conference. Its name honours William Taylor — the evangelist whose 1870 revival shaped Mumbai Methodism.

From its sanctuary, the Bishop leads the conference across seven districts and 36 congregations. The church remains a living landmark, hosting worship in multiple languages and standing at the heart of the Methodist community in Mumbai.

Its galleries include 23 historic photographs documenting the church's worship, fellowship, and mission through the years — a treasure of MRC's living heritage.

Historic Colaba

Bowen Memorial Methodist Church

Built in 1889 on Tullock Road in Colaba, the Bowen Memorial Methodist Church is one of Mumbai's most historic Methodist sanctuaries. Named in memory of the Bowen family, it stands as a fine example of late Victorian Gothic Revival architecture.

For over 135 years, the congregation has worshipped at this landmark — its soaring nave, memorial windows, and arched interior a serene witness amid the bustle of Apollo Bunder and the Gateway of India quarter.

Eight professional photographs of the church, including its striking façade and north tower elevation, are preserved in the archive.

Independence

1981: MCI Becomes Autonomous

The Methodist Church in Southern Asia had operated within the wider Methodist/United Methodist structure. During the 1970s, the church increasingly moved toward an autonomous Indian identity.

The 1976 Central Conference decided to consider an affiliated autonomous Methodist Church in India. A Constitution and plan were prepared through the Committee on Structure of Methodism and Church Union (COSMACU).

These were adopted in 1979. In 1980, the United Methodist Church's General Conference in Indianapolis granted the necessary enabling authority.

Finally, on 7 January 1981, the Methodist Church in India was formally reorganized and inaugurated as an Affiliated Autonomous Church of the United Methodist Church.

At the first General Conference in Madras (7–15 January 1981), four existing bishops chose to become bishops of MCI, and two more were elected, establishing the modern six-bishop structure.

Who We Are

MRC Today

The Mumbai Regional Conference is the modern administrative and episcopal successor to the historic Bombay Methodist work that grew from nineteenth-century Methodist revival activity.

Today, MRC encompasses a vibrant network of:

  • 34 Churches across Mumbai, Pune, Thane, Nagpur, and Nanded
  • 6 Languages: Tamil, English, Marathi, Kannada, Gujarati, Hindustani
  • 7 Districts including South Bombay, North Mumbai, and Pune
  • Educational institutions including Hutchings Schools
  • Active youth, women's, and men's ministries
